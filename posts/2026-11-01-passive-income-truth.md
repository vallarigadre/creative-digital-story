The first time my affiliate blog earned anything at all, I was so happy you'd think I'd just won something. It wasn't life-changing money. It was proof. Proof that a blog I don't sit down and write could actually earn while I was busy being a mom.

So let me tell you the truth about "passive income" from blogging, the phrase all of us typed into a search bar at some point. Mine is not 100% passive. It takes real work on certain days. It is slow. And I still get excited every time a post day comes around.

If you're wondering whether this kind of business can fit around a toddler, a job or a full life, here's exactly what mine takes, what I hand to AI, and what I never do.

## "Passive" is the wrong word. "Low-maintenance" is the right one

Here's how the money part works. My home and lifestyle blog, House & Habit, earns through Amazon affiliate links. When a reader buys a product I recommend, I earn a small commission. Nobody has to buy anything from me directly. The blog just needs to keep showing up with useful posts and good products.

The "showing up" part is where AI comes in. A scheduled Claude task drafts a new post every Tuesday and Friday, complete with product suggestions and photo prompts. That's the part that runs without me.

But a draft is not a published post. On those two days, I work. Not all day, not even close, but I work. That's why I've stopped using the word "passive." What I've built is low-maintenance: a business that needs a little of me, regularly, instead of a lot of me, all the time.

And honestly? Those are my favorite days. I love what I'm doing. When something feels like a chore, you quit. When you get a little thrill opening the draft, you keep going. That turns out to matter more than any tool.

## What a post day actually looks like

On a post day, it takes me less than two hours to get the blog post live and schedule the pins, the Threads posts and the Instagram posts that go with it. I do it in the morning, during my daughter's playtime.

The secret is that I'm not sitting at the laptop for those two hours. Most of my job is giving instructions and checking in on how it's going, which means I can step away, build a block tower, and come back.

Here's the order I work in:

1. **The draft arrives.** First thing I do is start the photos. I instruct AI to create the blog photos from the prompts in the draft, because image generation takes the longest and can run in the background.
2. **While the photos are being made, I read.** I go through the whole post, the title, the SEO title and everything around it. If something sounds off or vague, it gets fixed.
3. **AI uploads the post with the photos.** By the time I've finished reading, the post and its images are up as a draft on the site.
4. **I swap in my links and check every product.** This is the one I'll tell you more about in a second, because it's the most important part.
5. **Pins, Threads and Instagram.** AI makes the pin designs, the Threads write-ups and the Instagram carousel, and I direct the look and the words. Then they get scheduled.

That's it. Less than two hours, mostly in small pieces, with a toddler in the room. Which brings me to the part I refuse to automate.

## The part I never hand to AI: the final product picks

AI gives me product suggestions in every draft. I give the final ones.

When I swap in my own tracked links from Amazon's SiteStripe toolbar, I open each product and check it myself. I look at whether the link works, whether the price still makes sense, and whether there's a similar, better product available. If an AI-suggested product has a higher return rate, or it's not available anymore, I replace it personally.

Why be this picky? Because affiliate income is built on trust. If a reader buys a lamp I recommended and sends it back, I don't earn anything, and worse, they don't come back. The AI is fast at finding options. It doesn't know which product I'd actually feel good recommending to a friend. That's my job, and it's the reason the blog is mine.

If you're building your own, here's a prompt you can use to get AI to do the first pass of checking before you do the final one:

```
Here are the products in my blog post draft, with their Amazon links.
For each one, tell me:
1. Is it currently available, and roughly what does it cost right now?
2. Do the reviews mention returns, damage, or "not as pictured" often?
3. Is there a similar product with better reviews in the same price range?
Put the results in a table. Flag anything I should replace, and say why.
Don't guess: if you can't find the information, say so.
```

Then open the ones it flags and decide for yourself. The prompt saves time. The decision stays with you.

## The slow part nobody puts in the thumbnail

Here's the other truth: this does not make money overnight. It's slow. Really slow.

I opened the Pinterest account for this blog only recently, and Pinterest is where a lot of blog traffic comes from. My goal is a million monthly views. No AI on earth can make that happen in one night. Pins take time to be seen, posts take time to be found in search, and an affiliate blog needs a lot of readers before the small commissions add up.

There's also a practical hurdle worth knowing if you're starting with Amazon: new Associates generally need to make a few qualifying sales within their first 180 days to keep their account. So the early weeks matter, and it helps to have a way to get your first readers, like Pinterest, ready from day one.

My first money target is $1,000. Not $1,000 a week. Not a screenshot of a dashboard full of numbers. Just a clear first milestone that tells me the system works and is worth growing. Setting a small, honest target has kept me from comparing my month two to someone else's year five.

If you want to know what keeps me going through the slow part, it's not the money yet. It's the next thing.

## Why it gets a little addictive

Once you start doing something you actually like, it's hard to stop. I already have multiple blog sites in mind to get started. Each one would run the same way: AI drafts on a schedule, I review, I pick the products, I publish.

That's the quiet benefit of building a system instead of a single blog. The first one teaches you everything: the brief, the photos, the publishing, the promotion. The second one is mostly copying what already works and pointing it at a new topic.

Here's a quick checklist I'd use before starting any new blog in this system:

- **Do I like this topic enough to read about it twice a week?** You'll be reviewing every post.
- **Are there products people actually buy here?** Affiliate income needs real things to recommend.
- **Can it get traffic from Pinterest or search?** Visual, searchable topics are easier to grow.
- **Do I have two hours on post days for it?** Be honest about your calendar before adding another site.

## What low-maintenance gives me

Here's the real reason I built it this way. It gives me plenty of time and involvement with my little one.

Afternoon nap time, lunch time, play time: I'm there for all of it. My working morning happens alongside her play, in short check-ins rather than long, shut-the-door stretches. On the days without a post, the business mostly waits for me.

That's what I wanted when I started building income with AI. Not a business that runs with zero effort, because that doesn't exist. A business that fits around the life I actually want.

If you're looking for money with no effort, blogging isn't it, with or without AI. If you're looking for something that takes a couple of focused hours on a few days a week, grows slowly, and lets you keep your mornings and afternoons for your kid, it might be exactly it.
