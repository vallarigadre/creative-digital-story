Thanksgiving asks a lot of an outfit. It has to look good in the group photo, survive a warm kitchen and a cold porch, sit comfortably through a three-hour meal and still feel nice when you are curled up on the sofa for the game or the pie. Most of us solve that by pulling on whatever sweater is closest to the door. This year, let's do a little better than that, without giving up an ounce of comfort.

Thanksgiving falls on November 26 this year, which means you have a month to plan, shop and do a trial run. The good news is that fall 2026 fashion is practically designed for the holiday. Soft knits, easy waistbands, rich colors and one fun detail are exactly what the season's runways were about. Here is how to put them together into six outfits that work whether you are going to your in-laws, a Friendsgiving potluck or a long table at your aunt's house.

## Why Thanksgiving dressing feels different this year

The fall and winter collections leaned into two ideas that suit a holiday table perfectly. The first is quiet, pared-back dressing: classic shirts, clean skirts and washed cashmere with nothing fussy about them. The second is a playful, romantic streak, with bows, lace, animal prints and texture. Put the two together and you get the formula editors keep repeating this season: one statement piece, everything else simple.

Color is where things get exciting. Purple has been named the shade of the season in Who What Wear's runway roundup, from plum knits to whole outfits in one tone. It sits beautifully next to the browns, wines and creams you probably already own, and it looks lovely in candlelight. If purple feels like a lot, a deep wine or burgundy gives you the same richness in a quieter way.

Then there is texture. Suede, shearling, satin and leopard all turned up in the fall shows. These are fabrics that photograph well and feel special without needing a single sequin, which is exactly the level Thanksgiving calls for.

## The golden rule: dress for the whole day

Before we get to the outfits, a few ground rules that make every one of them work.

- **Pick a forgiving waist.** Elastic, a knit or a high, relaxed waistband. You should be able to sit through dinner and dessert without thinking about your outfit once.
- **Layer for two climates.** Kitchens run hot and porches run cold. A light top under a cardigan or jacket lets you adjust all day.
- **Mind your sleeves.** If you will be helping with the gravy or the dishes, choose sleeves that push up easily.
- **Choose shoes you can stand in.** Loafers, flat boots or a low block heel. You will be on your feet more than you think.
- **Lean on a print or a dark shade.** It is the easiest stain insurance there is.

## The Thanksgiving guest checklist

A great outfit is only half of being a wonderful guest. Run through this list the night before.

1. **Try the outfit on and sit down in it.** Five minutes on a chair tells you everything about a waistband.
2. **Pack a backup top.** Tuck a simple tee or sweater into your bag in case of a spill.
3. **Bring a stain pen.** It weighs nothing and saves the day.
4. **Plan your host gift.** A bottle of something, a candle or a loaf of good bread, wrapped and ready by the door.
5. **Lay out the jacket, bag and shoes together.** No hunting for the other loafer when you are already running late.
6. **Charge your phone.** Somebody has to take the group photo.
