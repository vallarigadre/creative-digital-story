By the end of this post you'll have a reusable way to make Pinterest pins with ChatGPT: one style note you paste once, one short prompt you fill in for each pin, and a set of tall pins that all look like they belong to the same brand. It's how I make the pins for this blog during my daughter's playtime, and you can make your first set in under an hour.

## What you need

- A ChatGPT account (the free plan can make images, with lower limits than the paid plans)
- A blog post, product or freebie you want the pin to link to
- A free Canva account, only for fixing text if ChatGPT gets a word wrong
- About 45 minutes

## Why ChatGPT works well for pins now

ChatGPT's image tool has become much better at short text. A headline of five to eight words usually comes out spelled correctly. Long sentences still go wrong sometimes, so the trick is to keep the words on your pin short.

It also lets you keep editing inside the same chat. If the background is too dark or the headline is too small, you just say so, and it adjusts that image instead of starting from scratch.

One thing to know before you start: Pinterest may show an "AI modified" label on pins it detects as AI-made. That's fine. What matters is that the pin is useful and leads to something worth clicking.

## Step by step

1. **Start one new chat for the whole batch.** Every pin in the same chat keeps the same colors, fonts and feel. A new chat for each pin is how you end up with ten pins that look like ten different brands.

2. **Paste the style note first.** This is the "brand brief" ChatGPT follows for every image. Copy mine below and change the colors and objects to suit your niche. Send it on its own and let ChatGPT reply before you ask for any images.

3. **Ask for one pin at a time with the pin prompt.** Fill in the three blanks: the headline, the one accent word and the small line underneath. Send it, wait for the image, then do the next one.

4. **Check every pin before you save it.** Read each word slowly. Check the size is tall (2:3, the shape Pinterest recommends, like 1000 x 1500 pixels). Check nothing important is squashed into a corner.

5. **Fix what's off with one short message.** "Make the headline bigger" or "the word 'beginners' is misspelled, fix just that" works better than rewriting the whole prompt.

6. **Download and name your files.** I name them after the post, like *my-post-pin-2.jpg*, so I always know which pin goes where.

7. **Upload to Pinterest with a keyword-rich title and description.** The image gets the click, but the words are what help Pinterest show your pin to people searching. If you're new to this part, my [Pinterest plan for a brand-new blog](/pinterest-for-new-blog) walks through boards and keywords.

## The style note (paste this first)

```
I'm making a set of Pinterest pins for my blog. Keep the same style in every image I ask for in this chat.

Size: tall 2:3 portrait (1024 x 1536), for Pinterest.
Look: a scrapbook-style collage of cut-out "sticker" photos with thin white outlines (a laptop, a coffee cup, an open planner, a phone, a few flowers) on a cream crumpled-paper background.
Headline: very large, in an elegant editorial serif font, with ONE word in a loose handwritten script as an accent.
Subline: a short line in small handwritten lettering under the headline.
Colors: burgundy, blush pink, cream and warm brown.
Footer: my website address printed small at the bottom center: yourwebsite.com
Rules: print all text exactly as I write it, in the exact spelling. No other words, no logos, no brand names. Leave clear space around the headline so it is easy to read on a phone.

Reply "ready" and wait for my first pin.
```

## The pin prompt (use this for every pin)

```
Pin [number]. Make this pin in the style above.
Headline (print exactly): "[5 to 8 words]"
Accent word in script: "[one word from the headline]"
Subline (print exactly): "[3 to 6 words]"
Main photo idea: [one object or scene that matches the topic, e.g. a laptop on a sofa with a mug]
```

Here's a real example you can try right now:

```
Pin 1. Make this pin in the style above.
Headline (print exactly): "Easy Dinners for Busy Weeknights"
Accent word in script: "Easy"
Subline (print exactly): "10 recipes, 30 minutes"
Main photo idea: a cozy kitchen counter with a bowl of pasta and a cutting board
```

Swap the words for your own topic and you have a pin. For your next pin, change the headline and the photo idea, and keep everything else. That's what makes a batch look like a set.

## Common snags (and quick fixes)

**A word is misspelled.** Ask once: "Fix only the spelling of '[word]', keep everything else the same." If it's still wrong, download the pin, open it in Canva, cover the word with a small shape in the background color and type it yourself. That takes two minutes.

**The pin came out square or wide.** Say "Make it tall, 2:3 portrait, for Pinterest." Putting the size in your style note usually stops this from happening at all.

**Every pin looks a bit different.** You probably started a new chat, or the style note got lost far up a long conversation. Paste the style note again and say "use this style for the next pins."

**The headline is too small to read on a phone.** Ask for "a much larger headline that fills the top third." Most people scroll Pinterest on their phone, so test it by looking at the pin on yours.

**It added extra words or a fake logo.** Remind it: "No other text and no logos, only the words I gave you."

**You hit the image limit.** On the free plan you may need to wait before making more. Make your most important pins first, the one for the post or product that matters most this week.
