Every white elephant party has the same two kinds of gifts. There is the one everybody fights over, and there is the one that gets quietly passed along to the person who lost the last round.

The difference is almost never the price. It is whether the gift is something a real person would be glad to take home. A soft throw gets stolen three times. A novelty gadget nobody understands does not.

So here is a smarter way to shop: gifts for the home, the kitchen and the bar, with a funny one or two thrown in, all chosen from the kinds of things that usually land well under $30. Think of this as your cheat sheet for walking into the party with the present everyone eyes.

## How a white elephant exchange works (and what to wrap)

If you are new to it, here is the short version. Everyone brings one wrapped gift under a set limit. Players draw numbers. Number one opens a gift. Number two can open a new one or steal number one's. Gifts usually get stolen a few times, then the game ends when the last person has gone.

A few practical rules make it run smoothly:

1. **Set the price limit in the invitation**, and keep it the same for everyone. Under $30 is a sweet spot.
2. **Limit steals to three per gift**, so the best present eventually settles.
3. **Wrap everything in the same plain paper** with a ribbon, so nobody can guess what is inside.
4. **Keep a small stack of backups** for the host, in case someone forgets theirs.

The gifts that get stolen are almost always cozy, useful or a little bit luxurious. That is what the rest of this post is built around.

## How to build a holiday gift basket

A gift basket is the secret weapon of a white elephant party, because it looks generous and still stays under the limit. The trick is to pick one theme and keep the pieces small.

**Cocoa night basket:** the [Hot Chocolate Gift Set with Reindeer Mug](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BKTJ2XM1?tag=creativedi03c-20) comes with sachets, mini marshmallows, candy canes and socks. Add a plaid napkin and a cinnamon stick for a basket that looks like it came from a boutique.

**Movie night basket:** a flannel throw, a bowl of popcorn kernels, a bottle of seasoning and a box of candy, packed in a shallow basket and tied with a ribbon.

**Happy hour basket:** the ice trays, a small bottle of bitters, a jar of cocktail cherries and a pack of cocktail napkins, set on a wooden board.

To build any of them, line the basket with tissue paper, put the tallest item at the back, tuck the soft things around the edges and finish with a ribbon and a handwritten tag.
